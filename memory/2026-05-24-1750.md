# Signal-Led Lead Discovery and Outreach System

## Core principle

The system should not start with "find businesses in a niche" as the main strategy.

The system should start with:

> Find commercially credible businesses where the website appears weaker than the business itself, and where there is a clear, specific improvement angle.

Industry and location are useful, but only as discovery lenses. They are not the actual strategy.

The real target is:

```text
strong business + weak digital presence + clear improvement angle
```

The system should prioritise finding **opportunity patterns**, not just businesses.

---

## 1. Strategic reframing

### Old model to avoid

```text
Pick industry
Pick location
Scrape businesses
Check websites
Decide who might need a new site
Generate outreach
```

This creates too much noise because the industry and location filters only reduce volume. They do not automatically create a sales reason.

### New model to follow

```text
Define opportunity pattern
Search through relevant business categories
Collect lightweight business records
Run programmatic qualification
Run website checks
Score business quality vs website quality
Use AI only for judgement and pattern synthesis
Send best leads to human review
Generate approved outreach
Track outcomes and refine
```

The system should be built around **qualified opportunities**, not raw leads.

A qualified opportunity means:

```text
The business appears credible.
The website has a measurable or visible weakness.
The weakness connects to a business outcome.
The offer can address that weakness efficiently.
There is a specific outreach angle.
```

---

## 2. Opportunity patterns

The system should support opportunity patterns as the main discovery strategy.

### Pattern 1: Hidden quality business

#### Description

A business appears reputable, active or high-quality, but its website does not communicate that quality well.

#### Common signals

```text
Strong reviews
Strong project photos
High-value services
Poor visual design
Weak mobile first impression
No strong trust section
No clear project gallery
No clear consultation/quote CTA
```

#### Best-fit industries

```text
cabinet makers
builders
landscapers
bathroom renovators
shopfitters
roof restorers
flooring installers
pool builders
signage companies
commercial fitout companies
```

#### Primary outreach angle

```text
Your work/reputation appears stronger than your website currently communicates.
```

---

### Pattern 2: Weak quote flow

#### Description

A business provides services that require qualification, but the website has a generic or unclear enquiry path.

#### Common signals

```text
High-value services
Generic contact form
No quote form
No booking flow
No service-specific intake
No click-to-call prominence on mobile
No fields for budget, timeline, service type, photos or location
```

#### Best-fit industries

```text
roofers
electricians
plumbers
HVAC
commercial cleaners
mechanics
security installers
pest control
trade services
```

#### Primary outreach angle

```text
Your site could do more to qualify serious enquiries before they reach you.
```

---

### Pattern 3: Underused proof

#### Description

The business has external proof, such as reviews, photos or reputation signals, but the website does not use those signals effectively.

#### Common signals

```text
Strong rating
High review count
Reviews not shown on website
Testimonials not obvious
Google proof not referenced
Project photos hidden or missing
No before/after examples
No case studies
```

#### Primary outreach angle

```text
Your strongest trust signals are not being used where customers are making the enquiry decision.
```

---

### Pattern 4: Premium service, low-trust website

#### Description

The business appears to sell high-value work, but the website presentation feels dated, cheap or generic.

#### Common signals

```text
Premium service keywords
High-value project types
Dated design
Weak typography/layout
Stock-looking imagery
Unclear positioning
Weak headline
No premium proof
No strong project examples
```

#### Primary outreach angle

```text
The website does not fully match the value or quality of the service being sold.
```

---

### Pattern 5: Weak service/search structure

#### Description

The business offers multiple services or serves multiple areas, but the website does not structure those services clearly.

#### Common signals

```text
Few or no service pages
Generic services page
No suburb/service-area pages
Poor page titles
Weak headings
No page-specific CTAs
No FAQs
No local relevance
```

#### Primary outreach angle

```text
Your services could be clearer for both search visibility and customer enquiries.
```

---

### Pattern 6: B2B RFQ friction

#### Description

A B2B supplier or industrial business has valuable products/services, but the website makes it hard for buyers to find information or request a quote.

#### Common signals

```text
Industrial or supplier category
Product/service categories unclear
No RFQ flow
No spec request path
No downloadable information
Messy navigation
No clear contact path
Old catalogue structure
```

#### Best-fit industries

```text
industrial suppliers
manufacturers
wholesalers
equipment suppliers
fabricators
packaging suppliers
safety equipment suppliers
commercial refrigeration
```

#### Primary outreach angle

```text
Buyers could have an easier path from finding the right service/product to requesting a quote.
```

## 3. Role of industry and location

Industry and location should not be treated as the core strategy.

They should be treated as **discovery lenses**.

### Industry is useful for

```text
Creating search query packs
Understanding common website failure patterns
Building reusable offer structures
Generating relevant audit criteria
Writing more specific outreach
Comparing similar businesses
Learning which categories respond
```

### Location is useful for

```text
Creating manageable market scans
Making outreach feel locally relevant
Comparing businesses in the same market
Identifying local SEO opportunities
Reducing duplicate/noisy results
Supporting suburb/service-area recommendations
```

### The system should not assume

```text
A business is a good lead just because it is in a chosen industry.
A business is a good lead just because it is in a chosen location.
A business with a bad website is automatically worth contacting.
```

The lead must still pass the opportunity criteria.

---

## 4. Recommended pipeline

Use a progressive qualification pipeline.

```text
Discovery
 ↓
Normalisation
 ↓
Deduplication
 ↓
Basic qualification
 ↓
Shallow website scan
 ↓
Programmatic signal extraction
 ↓
Rule-based scoring
 ↓
AI triage, only for promising leads
 ↓
Full AI audit, only for strong candidates
 ↓
Human review
 ↓
Approved outreach
 ↓
Follow-up and outcome tracking
```

The system should avoid sending every lead through AI.

The AI layer should only receive leads where programmatic signals suggest there is a likely opportunity.

## 5. Programmatic-first principle

Use code for anything factual, measurable, repeatable or deterministic.

Use AI only for subjective judgement, synthesis and persuasion.

Use human review for reputation, compliance and final quality control.

### Summary

```text
Code = evidence
AI = interpretation
Human = judgement
```

## 6. What should be programmatic

### Discovery

Programmatically:

```text
Generate search queries
Run source/API searches
Collect candidate records
Store source IDs
Record source query
Record discovery timestamp
Queue records for dedupe
```

The system should not use AI to decide whether a source result is a business unless it is ambiguous.

### Normalisation

Programmatically:

```text
Normalise URLs
Extract root domains
Resolve redirects
Standardise phone numbers
Clean business names
Standardise locations
Detect social-only URLs
Detect directory URLs
Detect parked or dead domains
```

### Deduplication

Programmatically dedupe by:

```text
source ID
place ID
domain
root domain
phone number
business name + suburb
business name + root domain
```

Use AI only for ambiguous duplicate cases.

### Basic qualification

Programmatically filter out:

```text
No website
Dead website
Wrong category
Low review count
Low rating
Franchise/chain if excluded
Marketplace/directory
Government/association site
Already contacted
Suppressed/unsubscribed
Recently audited
Social-only presence
```

### Website crawling

Programmatically crawl a limited set of pages:

```text
homepage
about page
services page
gallery/projects page
contact page
quote/booking page
testimonials/reviews page
```

Recommended limits:

```text
max pages: 8 to 12
max depth: 2
timeout: 10 to 15 seconds per page
```

### HTML and content extraction

Programmatically extract:

```text
title
meta description
H1

main navigation links

schema markup

CTA candidates

gallery/project keywords
testimonial/review keywords
quote/booking keywords
```

### Screenshot capture

Programmatically capture:

Also programmatically detect:

```

## 7. What AI should do

AI should not crawl, scrape, dedupe, extract HTML, run audits or send messages.

AI should interpret prepared evidence.

### AI should be used for

```text
Visual/design judgement
Mobile clarity judgement
Trust signal assessment
Business-context interpretation
Lead archetype classification
Opportunity angle generation
Mini-audit synthesis
Outreach message drafting
Follow-up message drafting
Objection response drafting
```

### AI should not be used for

```text
Finding contact details
Parsing HTML
Running PageSpeed
Checking whether a form exists
Checking whether a phone number exists
Deduping obvious records
Sending outreach automatically
Making final compliance decisions
Inventing issues
Making claims without evidence
```

## 8. AI gating model

Use a tiered AI system.

### Tier 0: No AI

Most leads should stop here.

Skip AI if:

```text
No website
Website unreachable
Wrong business type
Low review/business strength
Already has a strong modern site
No obvious offer fit
Franchise or unsuitable operator
Recently contacted
Suppressed/unsubscribed
```

### Tier 1: Cheap AI triage

Use text-only AI for leads where the programmatic layer sees some promise but the angle is unclear.

Input:

```text
Structured business data
Extracted website signals
Rule-based scores
Detected pages/forms/CTAs
No screenshots unless required
```

Output:

## 10. Scoring model

Use separate scores.

Do not use one blended score too early.

### Business Strength Score

Measures whether the business is worth pursuing.

Suggested signals:

```text
Strong rating
Meaningful review count
Recent reviews if available
High-value service keywords
Local service relevance
Project/proof indicators
Active profile/source signals
Clear commercial category
```

### Website Weakness Score

Measures whether the website has fixable gaps.

Suggested signals:

```text
Weak or missing primary CTA
No quote/booking path
Poor mobile presentation
Dated design
No prominent testimonials/reviews
No project gallery
Weak service page structure
Generic contact form
Poor performance
No local/service-area structure
```

### Offer Fit Score

Measures whether the weakness matches a service that can be sold efficiently.

Suggested signals:

```text
Matches known opportunity pattern
Can be improved with reusable website structure
Can be explained in one specific outreach angle
Concept preview would be easy to create
Likely business value is clear
```

### Opportunity Gap Score

Use:

```text
Opportunity Gap = Business Strength - Website Quality
```

Or:

```text
Opportunity Score = Business Strength + Website Weakness + Offer Fit
```

The preferred target is:

```text
High business strength
Medium/high website weakness
High offer fit
Clear primary angle
```

Avoid:

```text
Weak business + weak website
Strong business + strong website
Weak business + unclear offer fit
```

## 1. Strategic reframing

### Old model to avoid

```text
Pick industry
Pick location
Scrape businesses
Check websites
Decide who might need a new site
Generate outreach
```

This creates too much noise because the industry and location filters only reduce volume. They do not automatically create a sales reason.

### New model to follow

```text
Define opportunity pattern
Search through relevant business categories
Collect lightweight business records
Run programmatic qualification
Run website checks
Score business quality vs website quality
Use AI only for judgement and pattern synthesis
Send best leads to human review
Generate approved outreach
Track outcomes and refine
```

The system should be built around **qualified opportunities**, not raw leads.

A qualified opportunity means:

```text
The business appears credible.
The website has a measurable or visible weakness.
The weakness connects to a business outcome.
The offer can address that weakness efficiently.
There is a specific outreach angle.
```

## 2. Opportunity patterns

The system should support opportunity patterns as the main discovery strategy.

### Pattern 1: Hidden quality business

#### Description

A business appears reputable, active or high-quality, but its website does not communicate that quality well.

#### Common signals

```text
Strong reviews
Strong project photos
High-value services
Poor visual design
Weak mobile first impression
No strong trust section
No clear project gallery
No clear consultation/quote CTA
```

#### Best-fit industries

```text
cabinet makers
builders
landscapers
bathroom renovators
shopfitters
roof restorers
flooring installers
pool builders
signage companies
commercial fitout companies
```

#### Primary outreach angle

```text
Your work/reputation appears stronger than your website currently communicates.
```

---

### Pattern 2: Weak quote flow

#### Description

A business provides services that require qualification, but the website has a generic or unclear enquiry path.

#### Common signals

```text
High-value services
Generic contact form
No quote form
No booking flow
No service-specific intake
No click-to-call prominence on mobile
No fields for budget, timeline, service type, photos or location
```

#### Best-fit industries

```text
roofers
electricians
plumbers
HVAC
commercial cleaners
mechanics
security installers
pest control
trade services
```

#### Primary outreach angle

```text
Your site could do more to qualify serious enquiries before they reach you.
```

---

### Pattern 3: Underused proof

#### Description

The business has external proof, such as reviews, photos or reputation signals, but the website does not use those signals effectively.

#### Common signals

```text
Strong rating
High review count
Reviews not shown on website
Testimonials not obvious
Google proof not referenced
Project photos hidden or missing
No before/after examples
No case studies
```

#### Primary outreach angle

```text
Your strongest trust signals are not being used where customers are making the enquiry decision.
```

---

### Pattern 4: Premium service, low-trust website

#### Description

The business appears to sell high-value work, but the website presentation feels dated, cheap or generic.

#### Common signals

```text
Premium service keywords
High-value project types
Dated design
Weak typography/layout
Stock-looking imagery
Unclear positioning
Weak headline
No premium proof
No strong project examples
```

#### Primary outreach angle

```text
The website does not fully match the value or quality of the service being sold.
```

---

### Pattern 5: Weak service/search structure

#### Description

The business offers multiple services or serves multiple areas, but the website does not structure those services clearly.

#### Common signals

```text
Few or no service pages
Generic services page
No suburb/service-area pages
Poor page titles
Weak headings
No page-specific CTAs
No FAQs
No local relevance
```

#### Primary outreach angle

```text
Your services could be clearer for both search visibility and customer enquiries.
```

---

### Pattern 6: B2B RFQ friction

#### Description

A B2B supplier or industrial business has valuable products/services, but the website makes it hard for buyers to find information or request a quote.

#### Common signals

```text
Industrial or supplier category
Product/service categories unclear
No RFQ flow
No spec request path
No downloadable information
Messy navigation
No clear contact path
Old catalogue structure
```

#### Best-fit industries

```text
industrial suppliers
manufacturers
wholesalers
equipment suppliers
fabricators
packaging suppliers
safety equipment suppliers
commercial refrigeration
```

#### Primary outreach angle

```text
Buyers could have an easier path from finding the right service/product to requesting a quote.
```

## 3. Role of industry and location

Industry and location should not be treated as the core strategy.

They should be treated as **discovery lenses**.

### Industry is useful for

```text
Creating search query packs
Understanding common website failure patterns
Building reusable offer structures
Generating relevant audit criteria
Writing more specific outreach
Comparing similar businesses
Learning which categories respond
```

### Location is useful for

```text
Creating manageable market scans
Making outreach feel locally relevant
Comparing businesses in the same market
Identifying local SEO opportunities
Reducing duplicate/noisy results
Supporting suburb/service-area recommendations
```

### The system should not assume

```text
A business is a good lead just because it is in a chosen industry.
A business is a good lead just because it is in a chosen location.
A business with a bad website is automatically worth contacting.
```

The lead must still pass the opportunity criteria.

## 4. Recommended pipeline

Use a progressive qualification pipeline.

```text
Discovery
 ↓
Normalisation
 ↓
Deduplication
 ↓
Basic qualification
 ↓
Shallow website scan
 ↓
Programmatic signal extraction
 ↓
Rule-based scoring
 ↓
AI triage, only for promising leads
 ↓
Full AI audit, only for strong candidates
 ↓
Human review
 ↓
Approved outreach
 ↓
Follow-up and outcome tracking
```

The system should avoid sending every lead through AI.

The AI layer should only receive leads where programmatic signals suggest there is a likely opportunity.

## 5. Programmatic-first principle

Use code for anything factual, measurable, repeatable or deterministic.

Use AI only for subjective judgement, synthesis and persuasion.

Use human review for reputation, compliance and final quality control.

### Summary

```text
Code = evidence
AI = interpretation
Human = judgement
```

## 6. What should be programmatic

### Discovery

Programmatically:

```text
Generate search queries
Run source/API searches
Collect candidate records
Store source IDs
Record source query
Record discovery timestamp
Queue records for dedupe
```

The system should not use AI to decide whether a source result is a business unless it is ambiguous.

### Normalisation

Programmatically:

```text
Normalise URLs
Extract root domains
Resolve redirects
Standardise phone numbers
Clean business names
Standardise locations
Detect social-only URLs
Detect directory URLs
Detect parked or dead domains
```

### Deduplication

Programmatically dedupe by:

```text
source ID
place ID
domain
root domain
phone number
business name + suburb
business name + root domain
```

Use AI only for ambiguous duplicate cases.

### Basic qualification

Programmatically filter out:

```text
No website
Dead website
Wrong category
Low review count
Low rating
Franchise/chain if excluded
Marketplace/directory
Government/association site
Already contacted
Suppressed/unsubscribed
Recently audited
Social-only presence
```

### Website crawling

Programmatically crawl a limited set of pages:

```text
homepage
about page
services page
gallery/projects page
contact page
quote/booking page
testimonials/reviews page
```

Recommended limits:

```text
max pages: 8 to 12
max depth: 2
timeout: 10 to 15 seconds per page
```

### HTML and content extraction

Programmatically extract:

```text
title
meta description
H1
H2s
main navigation links
button text
link text
forms
input labels
textarea presence
submit buttons
phone numbers
email addresses
schema markup
page word count
image count
internal links
external links
social links
CTA candidates
service keywords
location keywords
gallery/project keywords
testimonial/review keywords
quote/booking keywords
```

### Screenshot capture

Programmatically capture:

```text
desktop homepage screenshot

contact/quote page screenshot if found
```

Also programmatically detect:

```text
horizontal overflow on mobile

blank page
visible error messages

schema detection
analytics/script detection
CMS/tech stack detection if useful
```

## 7. What AI should do

AI should not crawl, scrape, dedupe, extract HTML, run audits or send messages.

AI should interpret prepared evidence.

### AI should be used for

```text
Visual/design judgement
Mobile clarity judgement
Trust signal assessment
Business-context interpretation
Lead archetype classification
Opportunity angle generation
Mini-audit synthesis
Outreach message drafting
Follow-up message drafting
Objection response drafting
```

### AI should not be used for

```text
Finding contact details
Parsing HTML
Running PageSpeed
Checking whether a form exists
Checking whether a phone number exists
Deduping obvious records
Sending outreach automatically
Making final compliance decisions
Inventing issues
Making claims without evidence
```

## 8. AI gating model

Use a tiered AI system.

### Tier 0: No AI

Most leads should stop here.

Skip AI if:

```text
No website
Website unreachable
Wrong business type
Low review/business strength
Already has a strong modern site
No obvious offer fit
Franchise or unsuitable operator
Recently contacted
Suppressed/unsubscribed
```

### Tier 1: Cheap AI triage

Use text-only AI for leads where the programmatic layer sees some promise but the angle is unclear.

Input:

```text
Structured business data
Extracted website signals
Rule-based scores
Detected pages/forms/CTAs
No screenshots unless required
```

Output:

```json
{
 "should_deep_review": true,
 "lead_archetype": "hidden_quality_business",
 "primary_angle_candidate": "The business appears reputable, but the site does not make the enquiry path clear.",
 "confidence": 0.72,
 "reason": "Strong business signals and weak CTA/form structure."
}
```

### Tier 2: Full AI audit

Use full AI only for strong candidates.

Input:

```text
Structured evidence pack
Desktop screenshot
Mobile screenshot
Programmatic scores
Detected website gaps
Relevant opportunity patterns
```

Output:

```json
{
 "ai_confidence": 0.82,
 "lead_archetype": "hidden_quality_business",
 "primary_angle": "The business appears reputable, but the website does not communicate the quality of the work or make the enquiry path clear on mobile.",

 "Trust signals are not obvious on the homepage."

 "suggested_feature": "Project-based quote form",
 "should_contact": true,
 "reason_not_to_contact": null
}
```

### Tier 3: Human review

Only send strong candidates to human review.

The human should see:

```text

Screenshots

Website weakness score
Offer fit score

```

## 10. Scoring model

Use separate scores.

Do not use one blended score too early.

### Business Strength Score

Measures whether the business is worth pursuing.

Suggested signals:

```text
Strong rating
Meaningful review count
Recent reviews if available
High-value service keywords
Local service relevance
Project/proof indicators
Active profile/source signals
Clear commercial category
```

### Website Weakness Score

Measures whether the website has fixable gaps.

Suggested signals:

```text
Weak or missing primary CTA
No quote/booking path
Poor mobile presentation
Dated design
No prominent testimonials/reviews
No project gallery
Weak service page structure
Generic contact form
Poor performance
No local/service-area structure
```

### Offer Fit Score

Measures whether the weakness matches a service that can be sold efficiently.

Suggested signals:

```text
Matches known opportunity pattern
Can be improved with reusable website structure
Can be explained in one specific outreach angle
Concept preview would be easy to create
Likely business value is clear
```

### Opportunity Gap Score

Use:

```text
Opportunity Gap = Business Strength - Website Quality
```

Or:

```text
Opportunity Score = Business Strength + Website Weakness + Offer Fit
```

The preferred target is:

```text
High business strength
Medium/high website weakness
High offer fit
Clear primary angle
```

Avoid:

```text
Weak business + weak website
Strong business + strong website
Weak business + unclear offer fit
```

## 11. Suggested thresholds

Initial rules:

```text
If Business Strength < 50:
 skip

If Website Weakness < 30:
 skip or save as benchmark

If Offer Fit < 40:
 hold

If Business Strength >= 65 and Website Weakness >= 45:

If Business Strength >= 75 and Website Weakness >= 55 and Offer Fit >= 60:

These thresholds should be tuned from real outcomes.

## 16. Outreach rules

Outreach should be generated only after:

```text
programmatic qualification passed
AI review passed
human approval completed
contact source verified
suppression list checked
recent contact history checked
```

The outreach message should:

```text
mention the business specifically
include one genuine positive observation
describe one clear missed opportunity
avoid insulting the website
avoid saying "bad website"
avoid mentioning AI
focus on trust, enquiries, quote quality or reduced admin
ask a low-friction question
include sender identification
include a simple opt-out
```

The system should not sell "AI websites".

It should sell:

```text
modernisation
trust
better enquiries
clearer quote flow
reduced admin
better project presentation

## 18. Follow-up rules

```text

```text

```text

# Signal-Led Lead Discovery and Outreach System

## Core principle

The system should not start with "find businesses in a niche" as the main strategy.

The system should start with:

> Find commercially credible businesses where the website appears weaker than the business itself, and where there is a clear, specific improvement angle.

Industry and location are useful, but only as discovery lenses. They are not the actual strategy.

The real target is:

```text
strong business + weak digital presence + clear improvement angle
```

The system should prioritise finding **opportunity patterns**, not just businesses.

---

## 1. Strategic reframing

### Old model to avoid

```text
Pick industry
Pick location
Scrape businesses
Check websites
Decide who might need a new site
Generate outreach
```

This creates too much noise because the industry and location filters only reduce volume. They do not automatically create a sales reason.

### New model to follow

```text
Define opportunity pattern
Search through relevant business categories
Collect lightweight business records
Run programmatic qualification
Run website checks
Score business quality vs website quality
Use AI only for judgement and pattern synthesis
Send best leads to human review
Generate approved outreach
Track outcomes and refine
```

The system should be built around **qualified opportunities**, not raw leads.

A qualified opportunity means:

```text
The business appears credible.
The website has a measurable or visible weakness.
The weakness connects to a business outcome.
The offer can address that weakness efficiently.
There is a specific outreach angle.
```

---

The business has external proof, such as reviews, photos or reputation signals, but the website does not use those signals effectively.

#### Common signals

Strong rating
High review count
Reviews not shown on website
Testimonials not obvious
Google proof not referenced
Project photos hidden or missing
No before/after examples
No case studies
```

#### Primary outreach angle

```text
Your strongest trust signals are not being used where customers are making the enquiry decision.
```

### Pattern 4: Premium service, low-trust website

#### Description

The business appears to sell high-value work, but the website presentation feels dated, cheap or generic.

## 3. Role of industry and location

Industry and location should not be treated as the core strategy.

They should be treated as **discovery lenses**.

### Industry is useful for

```text

Building reusable offer structures
Generating relevant audit criteria
Writing more specific outreach
Comparing similar businesses
Learning which categories respond
```

### Location is useful for

```text
Creating manageable market scans
Making outreach feel locally relevant
Comparing businesses in the same market

```

### The system should not assume

```text
A business is a good lead just because it is in a chosen industry.
A business is a good lead just because it is in a chosen location.

The lead must still pass the opportunity criteria.

## 4. Recommended pipeline

```text

 ↓

 ↓

 ↓

 ↓

## 5. Programmatic-first principle

Use code for anything factual, measurable, repeatable or deterministic.

Use AI only for subjective judgement, synthesis and persuasion.

Use human review for reputation, compliance and final quality control.

### Summary

```text

AI = interpretation
Human = judgement

```text

```text

Avoid:

```text
Weak business + weak website

# Signal-Led Lead Discovery and Outreach System

## Core principle

The system should not start with "find businesses in a niche" as the main strategy.

The system should start with:

> Find commercially credible businesses where the website appears weaker than the business itself, and where there is a clear, specific improvement angle.

Industry and location are useful, but only as discovery lenses. They are not the actual strategy.

The real target is:

```text
strong business + weak digital presence + clear improvement angle
```

The system should prioritise finding **opportunity patterns**, not just businesses.

```text

Scrape businesses
Check websites
Decide who might need a new site
Generate outreach

Score business quality vs website quality
Use AI only for judgement and pattern synthesis
Send best leads to human review
Generate approved outreach
Track outcomes and refine

The business appears credible.

The offer can address that weakness efficiently.
There is a specific outreach angle.

Generating relevant audit criteria
Writing more specific outreach

```

### Location is useful for

```text

Comparing businesses in the same market

### The system should not assume

```text

## 5. Programmatic-first principle

Use AI only for subjective judgement, synthesis and persuasion.

Use human review for reputation, compliance and final quality control.

```text

## 10. Scoring model

Use separate scores.

Do not use one blended score too early.

### Business Strength Score

Measures whether the business is worth pursuing.

```text

Recent reviews if available
High-value service keywords
Local service relevance
Project/proof indicators
Active profile/source signals
Clear commercial category
```

### Website Weakness Score

Measures whether the website has fixable gaps.

Suggested signals:

```text
Weak or missing primary CTA
No quote/booking path
Poor mobile presentation
Dated design
No prominent testimonials/reviews
No project gallery
Weak service page structure
Generic contact form
Poor performance
No local/service-area structure
```

### Offer Fit Score

Measures whether the weakness matches a service that can be sold efficiently.

Suggested signals:

```text
Matches known opportunity pattern
Can be improved with reusable website structure
Can be explained in one specific outreach angle
Concept preview would be easy to create
Likely business value is clear
```

### Opportunity Gap Score

Use:

```text
Opportunity Gap = Business Strength - Website Quality
```

Or:

```text
Opportunity Score = Business Strength + Website Weakness + Offer Fit
```

The preferred target is:

```text
High business strength
Medium/high website weakness
High offer fit
Clear primary angle
```

Avoid:

```text
Weak business + weak website
Strong business + strong website
Weak business + unclear offer fit
```

# Signal-Led Lead Discovery and Outreach System

## Core principle

The system should not start with "find businesses in a niche" as the main strategy.

The system should start with:

> Find commercially credible businesses where the website appears weaker than the business itself, and where there is a clear, specific improvement angle.

Industry and location are useful, but only as discovery lenses. They are not the actual strategy.

The real target is:

```text
strong business + weak digital presence + clear improvement angle
```

The system should prioritise finding **opportunity patterns**, not just businesses.

---

## 1. Strategic reframing

### Old model to avoid

```text
Pick industry
Pick location
Scrape businesses
Check websites
Decide who might need a new site
Generate outreach
```

This creates too much noise because the industry and location filters only reduce volume. They do not automatically create a sales reason.

### New model to follow

```text
Define opportunity pattern
Search through relevant business categories
Collect lightweight business records
Run programmatic qualification
Run website checks
Score business quality vs website quality
Use AI only for judgement and pattern synthesis
Send best leads to human review
Generate approved outreach
Track outcomes and refine
```

The system should be built around **qualified opportunities**, not raw leads.

A qualified opportunity means:

```text
The business appears credible.
The website has a measurable or visible weakness.
The weakness connects to a business outcome.
The offer can address that weakness efficiently.
There is a specific outreach angle.
```

# Signal-Led Lead Discovery and Outreach System

## Core principle

The system should not start with "find businesses in a niche" as the main strategy.

The system should start with:

> Find commercially credible businesses where the website appears weaker than the business itself, and where there is a clear, specific improvement angle.

Industry and location are useful, but only as discovery lenses. They are not the actual strategy.

The real target is:

```text
strong business + weak digital presence + clear improvement angle
```

The system should prioritise finding **opportunity patterns**, not just businesses.

---

## 1. Strategic reframing

### Old model to avoid

```text
Pick industry
Pick location
Scrape businesses
Check websites
Decide who might need a new site
Generate outreach
```

This creates too much noise because the industry and location filters only reduce volume. They do not automatically create a sales reason.

### New model to follow

```text
Define opportunity pattern
Search through relevant business categories
Collect lightweight business records
Run programmatic qualification
Run website checks
Score business quality vs website quality
Use AI only for judgement and pattern synthesis
Send best leads to human review
Generate approved outreach
Track outcomes and refine
```

The system should be built around **qualified opportunities**, not raw leads.

A qualified opportunity means:

```text
The business appears credible.
The website has a measurable or visible weakness.
The weakness connects to a business outcome.
The offer can address that weakness efficiently.
There is a specific outreach angle.
```

---

Suggested signals:

```text

Can be explained in one specific outreach angle
Concept preview would be easy to create
Likely business value is clear
```

Use:

```text
Opportunity Gap = Business Strength - Website Quality
```

```text
Opportunity Score = Business Strength + Website Weakness + Offer Fit

```text

Medium/high website weakness
High offer fit
Clear primary angle
```

Avoid:

```text
Weak business + weak website

Weak business + unclear offer fit

# Signal-Led Lead Discovery and Outreach System

## Core principle

The system should not start with "find businesses in a niche" as the main strategy.

The system should start with:

> Find commercially credible businesses where the website appears weaker than the business itself, and where there is a clear, specific improvement angle.

Industry and location are useful, but only as discovery lenses. They are not the actual strategy.

The real target is:

```text
strong business + weak digital presence + clear improvement angle
```

The system should prioritise finding **opportunity patterns**, not just businesses.

---

## 1. Strategic reframing

### Old model to avoid

```text
Pick industry
Pick location
Scrape businesses
Check websites
Decide who might need a new site
Generate outreach
```

This creates too much noise because the industry and location filters only reduce volume. They do not automatically create a sales reason.

### New model to follow

```text
Define opportunity pattern
Search through relevant business categories
Collect lightweight business records
Run programmatic qualification
Run website checks
Score business quality vs website quality
Use AI only for judgement and pattern synthesis
Send best leads to human review
Generate approved outreach
Track outcomes and refine
```

The system should be built around **qualified opportunities**, not raw leads.

A qualified opportunity means:

```text
The business appears credible.
The website has a measurable or visible weakness.
The weakness connects to a business outcome.
The offer can address that weakness efficiently.
There is a specific outreach angle.
```

---

## 2. Opportunity patterns

The system should support opportunity patterns as the main discovery strategy.

### Pattern 1: Hidden quality business

#### Description

A business appears reputable, active or high-quality, but its website does not communicate that quality well.

#### Common signals

```text

High-value services
Poor visual design

```

```text
cabinet makers
builders
landscapers
bathroom renovators
shopfitters
roof restorers
flooring installers
pool builders
signage companies
commercial fitout companies
```

#### Primary outreach angle

```text
Your work/reputation appears stronger than your website currently communicates.
```

---

### Pattern 2: Weak quote flow

#### Description

A business provides services that require qualification, but the website has a generic or unclear enquiry path.

#### Common signals

```text
High-value services
Generic contact form
No quote form
No booking flow
No service-specific intake
No click-to-call prominence on mobile

#### Best-fit industries

roofers
electricians
plumbers
HVAC
commercial cleaners
mechanics
security installers
pest control
trade services
```

#### Primary outreach angle

```text
Your site could do more to qualify serious enquiries before they reach you.
```

No before/after examples
No case studies
```

# Signal-Led Lead Discovery and Outreach System

## Core principle

The system should not start with "find businesses in a niche" as the main strategy.

The system should start with:

> Find commercially credible businesses where the website appears weaker than the business itself, and where there is a clear, specific improvement angle.

Industry and location are useful, but only as discovery lenses. They are not the actual strategy.

The real target is:

```text
strong business + weak digital presence + clear improvement angle
```

The system should prioritise finding **opportunity patterns**, not just businesses.

---

## 1. Strategic reframing

### Old model to avoid

```text
Pick industry
Pick location
Scrape businesses
Check websites
Decide who might need a new site
Generate outreach
```

This creates too much noise because the industry and location filters only reduce volume. They do not automatically create a sales reason.

### New model to follow

```text
Define opportunity pattern
Search through relevant business categories
Collect lightweight business records
Run programmatic qualification
Run website checks
Score business quality vs website quality
Use AI only for judgement and pattern synthesis
Send best leads to human review
Generate approved outreach
Track outcomes and refine
```

The system should be built around **qualified opportunities**, not raw leads.

A qualified opportunity means:

```text
The business appears credible.
The website has a measurable or visible weakness.
The weakness connects to a business outcome.
The offer can address that weakness efficiently.
There is a specific outreach angle.
```

---

## 2. Opportunity patterns

The system should support opportunity patterns as the main discovery strategy.

### Pattern 1: Hidden quality business

#### Description

A business appears reputable, active or high-quality, but its website does not communicate that quality well.

#### Common signals

shopfitters
roof restorers
flooring installers
pool builders
signage companies
commercial fitout companies
```

#### Primary outreach angle

```text
Your work/reputation appears stronger than your website currently communicates.
```

#### Description

A business provides services that require qualification, but the website has a generic or unclear enquiry path.

#### Common signals

```text

Industry and location should not be treated as the core strategy.

They should be treated as **discovery lenses**.

### Industry is useful for

```text

```

```text

The lead must still pass the opportunity criteria.

# Signal-Led Lead Discovery and Outreach System

## Core principle

The system should not start with "find businesses in a niche" as the main strategy.

The system should start with:

> Find commercially credible businesses where the website appears weaker than the business itself, and where there is a clear, specific improvement angle.

Industry and location are useful, but only as discovery lenses. They are not the actual strategy.

The real target is:

```text
strong business + weak digital presence + clear improvement angle
```

The system should prioritise finding **opportunity patterns**, not just businesses.

---

## 1. Strategic reframing

### Old model to avoid

```text
Pick industry
Pick location
Scrape businesses
Check websites
Decide who might need a new site
Generate outreach
```

This creates too much noise because the industry and location filters only reduce volume. They do not automatically create a sales reason.

### New model to follow

```text
Define opportunity pattern
Search through relevant business categories
Collect lightweight business records
Run programmatic qualification
Run website checks
Score business quality vs website quality
Use AI only for judgement and pattern synthesis
Send best leads to human review
Generate approved outreach
Track outcomes and refine
```

The system should be built around **qualified opportunities**, not raw leads.

A qualified opportunity means:

```text
The business appears credible.
The website has a measurable or visible weakness.
The weakness connects to a business outcome.
The offer can address that weakness efficiently.
There is a specific outreach angle.
```

---

## 2. Opportunity patterns

The system should support opportunity patterns as the main discovery strategy.

### Pattern 1: Hidden quality business

#### Description

A business appears reputable, active or high-quality, but its website does not communicate that quality well.

#### Common signals

```text

Strong project photos
High-value services
Poor visual design
Weak mobile first impression
No strong trust section

No clear consultation/quote CTA

```text
cabinet makers
builders
landscapers
bathroom renovators
shopfitters
roof restorers
flooring installers
pool builders
signage companies
commercial fitout companies
```

#### Primary outreach angle

```text
Your work/reputation appears stronger than your website currently communicates.
```

---

#### Description

A business provides services that require qualification, but the website has a generic or unclear enquiry path.

#### Common signals

```text
High-value services

```

#### Primary outreach angle

Your site could do more to qualify serious enquiries before they reach you.
```

## 3. Role of industry and location

Industry and location should not be treated as the core strategy.

They should be treated as **discovery lenses**.

### Industry is useful for

```text
Creating search query packs
Understanding common website failure patterns
Building reusable offer structures
Generating relevant audit criteria
Writing more specific outreach
Comparing similar businesses
Learning which categories respond
```

### Location is useful for

```text
Creating manageable market scans
Making outreach feel locally relevant
Comparing businesses in the same market
Identifying local SEO opportunities
Reducing duplicate/noisy results
Supporting suburb/service-area recommendations
```

### The system should not assume

```text
A business is a good lead just because it is in a chosen industry.
A business is a good lead just because it is in a chosen location.
A business with a bad website is automatically worth contacting.
```

The lead must still pass the opportunity criteria.

Human = judgement

# Signal-Led Lead Discovery and Outreach System

## Core principle

The system should not start with "find businesses in a niche" as the main strategy.

The system should start with:

> Find commercially credible businesses where the website appears weaker than the business itself, and where there is a clear, specific improvement angle.

Industry and location are useful, but only as discovery lenses. They are not the actual strategy.

The real target is:

```text
strong business + weak digital presence + clear improvement angle
```

The system should prioritise finding **opportunity patterns**, not just businesses.

---

## 1. Strategic reframing

### Old model to avoid

```text
Pick industry
Pick location
Scrape businesses
Check websites
Decide who might need a new site
Generate outreach
```

This creates too much noise because the industry and location filters only reduce volume. They do not automatically create a sales reason.

### New model to follow

```text
Define opportunity pattern
Search through relevant business categories
Collect lightweight business records
Run programmatic qualification
Run website checks
Score business quality vs website quality
Use AI only for judgement and pattern synthesis
Send best leads to human review
Generate approved outreach
Track outcomes and refine
```

The system should be built around **qualified opportunities**, not raw leads.

A qualified opportunity means:

```text
The business appears credible.
The website has a measurable or visible weakness.
The weakness connects to a business outcome.
The offer can address that weakness efficiently.
There is a specific outreach angle.
```

---

## 2. Opportunity patterns

The system should support opportunity patterns as the main discovery strategy.

### Pattern 1: Hidden quality business

#### Description

A business appears reputable, active or high-quality, but its website does not communicate that quality well.

#### Common signals

```text

Strong project photos
High-value services
Poor visual design
Weak mobile first impression
No strong trust section
No clear project gallery
No clear consultation/quote CTA
```

#### Best-fit industries

```text
cabinet makers
builders
landscapers
bathroom renovators
shopfitters
roof restorers
flooring installers
pool builders
signage companies
commercial fitout companies
```

#### Primary outreach angle

```text
Your work/reputation appears stronger than your website currently communicates.
```

---

### Pattern 2: Weak quote flow

#### Description

A business provides services that require qualification, but the website has a generic or unclear enquiry path.

#### Common signals

```text
High-value services
Generic contact form

No service-specific intake

commercial cleaners
mechanics
security installers
pest control
trade services
```

#### Primary outreach angle

```text
Your site could do more to qualify serious enquiries before they reach you.
```

No before/after examples

Your strongest trust signals are not being used where customers are making the enquiry decision.
```

## 3. Role of industry and location

Industry and location should not be treated as the core strategy.

They should be treated as **discovery lenses**.

### Industry is useful for

```text

Building reusable offer structures
Generating relevant audit criteria
Writing more specific outreach
Comparing similar businesses
Learning which categories respond
```

### Location is useful for

```text
Creating manageable market scans
Making outreach feel locally relevant
Comparing businesses in the same market
Identifying local SEO opportunities
Reducing duplicate/noisy results
Supporting suburb/service-area recommendations
```

### The system should not assume

```text
A business is a good lead just because it is in a chosen industry.
A business is a good lead just because it is in a chosen location.
A business with a bad website is automatically worth contacting.
```

The lead must still pass the opportunity criteria.

## 10. Scoring model

Use separate scores.

Do not use one blended score too early.

### Business Strength Score

Measures whether the business is worth pursuing.

Suggested signals:

```text
Strong rating
Meaningful review count
Recent reviews if available
High-value service keywords
Local service relevance
Project/proof indicators
Active profile/source signals
Clear commercial category
```

### Website Weakness Score

Measures whether the website has fixable gaps.

Suggested signals:

```text
Weak or missing primary CTA
No quote/booking path
Poor mobile presentation
Dated design
No prominent testimonials/reviews
No project gallery
Weak service page structure
Generic contact form
Poor performance
No local/service-area structure
```

### Offer Fit Score

Measures whether the weakness matches a service that can be sold efficiently.

Suggested signals:

```text
Matches known opportunity pattern
Can be improved with reusable website structure
Can be explained in one specific outreach angle
Concept preview would be easy to create
Likely business value is clear
```

### Opportunity Gap Score

Use:

```text
Opportunity Gap = Business Strength - Website Quality
```

Or:

```text
Opportunity Score = Business Strength + Website Weakness + Offer Fit
```

The preferred target is:

```text
High business strength
Medium/high website weakness
High offer fit
Clear primary angle
```

Avoid:

```text
Weak business + weak website
Strong business + strong website
Weak business + unclear offer fit
```

# Signal-Led Lead Discovery and Outreach System

## Core principle

The system should not start with "find businesses in a niche" as the main strategy.

The system should start with:

> Find commercially credible businesses where the website appears weaker than the business itself, and where there is a clear, specific improvement angle.

Industry and location are useful, but only as discovery lenses. They are not the actual strategy.

The real target is:

```text
strong business + weak digital presence + clear improvement angle
```

The system should prioritise finding **opportunity patterns**, not just businesses.

---

## 1. Strategic reframing

### Old model to avoid

```text
Pick industry
Pick location
Scrape businesses
Check websites
Decide who might need a new site
Generate outreach
```

This creates too much noise because the industry and location filters only reduce volume. They do not automatically create a sales reason.

### New model to follow

```text
Define opportunity pattern
Search through relevant business categories
Collect lightweight business records
Run programmatic qualification
Run website checks
Score business quality vs website quality
Use AI only for judgement and pattern synthesis
Send best leads to human review
Generate approved outreach
Track outcomes and refine
```

The system should be built around **qualified opportunities**, not raw leads.

A qualified opportunity means:

```text
The business appears credible.
The website has a measurable or visible weakness.
The weakness connects to a business outcome.
The offer can address that weakness efficiently.
There is a specific outreach angle.
```

---

## 2. Opportunity patterns

The system should support opportunity patterns as the main discovery strategy.

### Pattern 1: Hidden quality business

#### Description

A business appears reputable, active or high-quality, but its website does not communicate that quality well.

#### Common signals

shopfitters
roof restorers
flooring installers
pool builders
signage companies
commercial fitout companies
```

#### Primary outreach angle

```text
Your work/reputation appears stronger than your website currently communicates.
```

A business provides services that require qualification, but the website has a generic or unclear enquiry path.

Industry and location should not be treated as the core strategy.

## 10. Scoring model

### Website Weakness Score

Measures whether the website has fixable gaps.

Suggested signals:

```text

Poor mobile presentation
Dated design
No prominent testimonials/reviews
No project gallery
Weak service page structure
Generic contact form
Poor performance
No local/service-area structure
```

### Offer Fit Score

Measures whether the weakness matches a service that can be sold efficiently.

Suggested signals:

```text
Matches known opportunity pattern
Can be improved with reusable website structure
Can be explained in one specific outreach angle
Concept preview would be easy to create
Likely business value is clear
```

### Opportunity Gap Score

Use:

```text
Opportunity Gap = Business Strength - Website Quality
```

Or:

```text
Opportunity Score = Business Strength + Website Weakness + Offer Fit
```

The preferred target is:

```text
High business strength
Medium/high website weakness
High offer fit
Clear primary angle
```

Avoid:

```text
Weak business + weak website
Strong business + strong website
Weak business + unclear offer fit
```

For lower-confidence leads, ask whether they would like to see the concept first.

## 18. Follow-up rules

Follow-ups should stop after 3 attempts.

Suggested cadence:

```text
3 business days
7 business days
14 business days
```

Each follow-up should add a new useful observation.

Avoid generic "just following up" messages.

Follow-up themes:

```text
review proof not used clearly
quote flow could better qualify serious enquiries
competitor/service comparison
mobile CTA could be clearer
project gallery could work harder
```

Stop immediately on:

```text
reply
unsubscribe
negative response
bounce
manual suppression
```

```

The system should optimise around response and conversion data, not assumptions.

## 21. Preferred implementation philosophy

Build a **lead refinery**, not a lead warehouse.

Avoid:

```text
Scrape huge database
Store everything
Send everything through AI
Generate generic audits
Automate cold outreach too early
```

Prefer:

```text
Discover broadly
Store lightly
Filter aggressively
Audit progressively
Use AI selectively
Review manually
Outreach carefully
Learn from outcomes
```

## 22. Final operating rule

The system should only contact a business when it can complete this sentence with evidence:

```text
This business is worth contacting because it appears commercially credible, its website has a specific weakness, and that weakness creates a clear opportunity we can help improve.
```

If the system cannot complete that sentence, the lead should not move to outreach.

Final model:

```text
Code collects evidence.
Rules filter and score.
AI interprets the strongest candidates.
Human approves the actual contact.
Outcomes refine the system.
```
